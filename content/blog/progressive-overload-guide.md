# Training hard enough, not blindly

Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed on the body during training. Without it, muscles lack the stimulus to adapt. With too much, fatigue outruns recovery.

FitTrack tracks overload through **weight, reps, sets, and RPE (rate of perceived exertion)** — converting subjective effort into actionable progression cues on the [workout](/workout) screen.

## RPE and RIR: a practical pair

**RPE** rates how hard a set felt on a 1–10 scale (for working sets, practitioners typically use 6–10). **RIR (reps in reserve)** counts how many more reps you could have completed:

**RIR = 10 − RPE**

Zourdos MC, Klemp A, Dolan C, et al. (2016) validated RPE as a proxy for repetitions in reserve across experienced lifters. Their work supports using RPE to auto-regulate load when daily readiness fluctuates — a more nuanced approach than adding 2.5 kg every session regardless of sleep or stress.

Helms ER, Cronin J, Storey A, et al. (2016) reviewed RPE-based resistance-training prescription and recommended **RIR 1–3** (RPE 7–9) for hypertrophy: close enough to failure to drive adaptation, far enough from it to manage fatigue across weekly volume.

## How FitTrack uses RPE for progression

When you log a set in [workout](/workout), record weight, reps, and RPE. FitTrack compares that performance to your program prescription and suggests the next step:

- **RPE ≤ 7 with reps to spare** — increase load (~2.5%) while holding target reps.
- **RPE ≥ 9 below target reps** — hold weight and consolidate.
- **Otherwise** — add a rep at the same load before jumping weight.

This mirrors autoregulated progressive overload: progression follows demonstrated capacity, not the calendar.

Last-session context appears inline ("Last time: 80 kg × 8 @ RPE 8") so you do not guess what to load on the bar.

## Volume is part of overload

Overload is not only heavier weight. **Volume load** (sets × reps × weight) accumulates weekly stress. Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW (2017) showed a dose-response between weekly set volume and hypertrophy — more sets generally produce more growth up to a practical ceiling.

FitTrack calculates volume per session and compares weekly totals against evidence-based guidelines. Read [How Much Training Volume Do You Need?](/blog/training-volume) for the research behind those ranges.

## Logging workflow

1. Start a session from [workout](/workout) and select your program day.
2. Log each set with weight, reps, and RPE immediately after completion.
3. Review progression suggestions before your next exposure to the same lift.
4. Check [progress](/progress) for rolling strength and volume trends.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Better approach |
|---------|-----------------|
| Training every set to failure (RPE 10) | Target RIR 1–3 on most hypertrophy work (Helms et al., 2016) |
| Adding weight when form breaks down | Hold load until target reps at RPE ≤ 8 (Zourdos et al., 2016) |
| Ignoring RPE on good days | Log it — easy sets at low RPE signal readiness to progress |
| Chasing load without tracking volume | Monitor weekly sets per muscle group |
